A 400-year-old craft, a supply chain nobody can see

Most Chikankari reaches buyers after passing through several hands — the karigar, the workshop, a wholesaler in Chowk, an agent, an exporter. By the time it reaches a store abroad, nobody can say who made it, and machine-made copies sit on the same shelf at the same price.

The Craft

Every flower is a stitch with a name

Chikankari is white-on-white embroidery from Lucknow, traditionally worked on fine cotton mulmul, with its origins usually traced to the Mughal court. Karigars draw on a vocabulary of around thirty-two stitches. These are the ones you will see most often.

    Our Story

    It started with walking the lanes of Lucknow

    Karigariandaz began as a field study by students at Sitare University. We wanted to know one thing: can Lucknow's Chikankari reach buyers outside India without losing the people who make it along the way?

    So we went and asked. We visited workshops and shops in and around Lucknow, sat with owners who explained how orders, wages and middlemen really work, and spent days in the wholesale lanes of Chowk. Some welcomed us in; a couple of them didn't want to talk. All of it shaped what we built.

    The name says what we care about: कारीगरी — the skill of the karigar — and अंदाज़ — the style that makes Lucknow's work unlike anyone else's.
